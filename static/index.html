Schematic in, breadboard out.

Hand it a screenshot from LTspice, PSpice or KiCad. It reads what connects to what, then works out where every part sits on a real 830-point board — which holes, which way round, and every jumper you have to cut — and walks you through building it one step at a time.

About

1 Draw the schematic

Draw it the way you would in LTspice: place parts, wire pin to pin, add ground and name nets with labels. No simulation. The drawing becomes a netlist directly, so nothing gets guessed.

2 Pick the real parts

A schematic symbol doesn't have a pinout — the package does. Choose what you're actually putting in the board; everything downstream depends on it.

Breadboard

Drag the slider, or press Play, to build the board one part at a time.

Hover anything: rails show their supply, ICs and transistors show their pinout and which way round they go, passives show their value and colour code. Hovering also lights up everything else on the same net — every point the circuit treats as one electrical node.
